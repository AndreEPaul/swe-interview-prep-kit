# Professional presence: clear, consistent, and low-maintenance

Maintaining an online profile can feel like another job. This is not a requirement to build a personal brand, post regularly, publish side projects, or be active everywhere. A modest, accurate presence can help someone discover you, understand your work, and decide whether to reach out.

Treat it as a supplement to applications, referrals, and interview preparation, not a measure of engineering ability or a guarantee of recruiter attention. Private work and a quiet public profile are not evidence of weak skills.

## Choose a useful surface, not every surface

| Surface | A small useful version |
| --- | --- |
| Professional network profile, such as LinkedIn | Understandable headline/introduction, accurate experience, relevant skills, and an appropriate way to contact you. |
| Personal website | Short introduction, selected experience or permitted work samples, and links/contact details you choose to make public. |
| Portfolio | A few permitted examples explaining the problem, your contribution, decisions, and limits, not just screenshots or technology badges. |
| Public GitHub profile | Clear profile text and a few representative repositories with readable setup/purpose notes, if you have work you want and are permitted to share. |

Start with the surface relevant to how you want to be found. A professional network can be sufficient; a website is optional, and not everyone needs public code. Do not create a contribution streak or open-source project solely to look employable.

## Make the first impression understandable

A reader should be able to answer:

1. What kind of work does this person do?
2. What experience or strengths support that description?
3. Where can I learn more or contact them, if they want that?

### Headline or short introduction

Describe your actual focus in language an outsider recognizes. Include relevant technical terms where truthful and useful; do not stack every tool, inflate a title, or chase an assumed search algorithm.

**Synthetic example, not a candidate claim:**

> Frontend engineer building customer-facing products with React and TypeScript, with experience in API integration and technical delivery.

Use your own wording. A platform's headline need not duplicate your job title exactly, but it should not imply a role, level, or expertise you do not have.

### About or summary

A short natural introduction can describe the work you enjoy, the problems you have worked on, and a few concrete strengths. It can be more conversational than a resume. Keep your own voice; AI can help clarify a draft without turning it into interchangeable promotional copy.

You do not need a grand career mission, a public job-search announcement, or personal details you would rather not share.

### Experience and skills

- Make roles, promotions, employer transitions, and dates consistent with the resume you actually use.
- Describe what the product/work did, your contribution, and a useful outcome or capability. Replace internal jargon with enough context for an outsider.
- Keep personal ownership, team scope, pilot/test boundaries, and measured versus estimated benefits accurate.
- Select skills that reflect real experience and the work you want to discuss. Do not imply equal depth in every technology or add fashionable terms without evidence.

Consistency is not verbatim duplication. A resume is selective for a role; a profile may give broader context, and a portfolio can go deeper. They should still tell a compatible factual story.

Use the [resume guide](./resume.md) for evidence, attribution, and voice checks. If adapting text with an assistant, provide the selected profile excerpts and an accurate resume or fact summary; do not assume it knows the latest dates or achievements.

## Evidence is optional; permission is not

If you have permitted work samples, make them understandable:

- Explain the problem, your role versus collaborators, a decision, and what was verified.
- Give a repository a clear purpose and setup path if you want people to run it.
- Label a prototype, learning exercise, synthetic example, or AI-assisted project honestly.

Do not publish employer code, customer data, internal screenshots, private assessments, or credentials to prove you did meaningful work. A high-level permitted description can be enough. Absence of public examples does not need an apology.

Review visibility, activity-notification, and contact settings before editing. Choose what is appropriate for your circumstances; making updates need not mean broadcasting a job search. Use only contact details you are comfortable exposing, and keep private drafts/reviews outside this repository. An assistant should propose changes, not log in, publish, message people, or expand access without an explicit request.

## A bounded refresh, not an ongoing obligation

One possible short pass:

1. Read the headline and introduction as someone unfamiliar with your employer.
2. Fix stale titles/dates and clarify one or two important experience descriptions.
3. Review the most relevant skills and any selected links/work samples.
4. Check what is visible publicly and how a legitimate recruiter can contact you.
5. Ask a trusted reader what work and strengths they understood, then stop.

You do not need to overhaul every section at once. Revisit after a meaningful role/project change or when your target changes, rather than maintaining a posting quota.

## Notice results without promising causation

Some job seekers report more recruiter outreach after improving their profiles. That is a useful reason to try a small refresh, not proof of a universal effect or that one edit caused the change.

If you want to assess it, note the date and broad changes, then observe the relevance of inbound opportunities as well as message volume. Market conditions, networks, role demand, and platform changes also affect outreach. No activity schedule, keyword formula, or profile score guarantees interviews.

The goal is a clear, truthful, sustainable representation of your work, not a new identity you have to perform online.
